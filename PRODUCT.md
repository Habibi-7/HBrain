# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Hvis is for a single local-first power user who wants a trustworthy daily view of how time is spent across apps, websites, and habits. The user is usually checking it during or after a work session, with the goal of changing behavior rather than admiring analytics.

## Product Purpose

Hvis exists to turn local activity data into a calm behavior-change dashboard. It should make the day understandable at a glance, reveal patterns that matter, and support small corrective actions through habits, categories, and later integrations. Success means the user can quickly answer what pulled their attention, what supported their goals, and what to do next.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, sharp, disciplined. The product should feel closer to Linear or Raycast than a generic SaaS analytics dashboard: restrained, precise, fast, and confident without decoration.

## Anti-references

Do not mimic ActivityWatch's default UI: plain, dated, utilitarian, and visually flat. Avoid generic SaaS dashboards with purple metric cards, noisy grids, and decorative data panels. Avoid gamified streak casino patterns, badges, confetti, or moralizing nudges. Avoid enterprise/admin energy: heavy tables, bland gray panels, and dense configuration surfaces as the default.

## Design Principles

1. Clarity changes behavior: every screen should help the user notice one meaningful pattern or take one small next action.
2. Restraint earns trust: use quiet hierarchy, careful spacing, and precise labels instead of decoration.
3. Local data should feel tangible: app, tab, category, and habit data should read as lived time, not abstract telemetry.
4. Calm beats gamification: streaks and habits can motivate, but never become noisy or manipulative.
5. The tool should disappear: interactions should be fast, familiar, keyboard-friendly, and consistent across screens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and interactive controls. Preserve visible keyboard focus, sensible hit targets, and reduced-motion support. Prefer minimal motion: use state transitions and subtle feedback, not page-load choreography or animated entrances.
